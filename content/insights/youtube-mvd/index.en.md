## Watch: The Blood Vessel Compressing the Nerve

In this video, I show the surgery itself — microvascular decompression, where I reposition a blood vessel that is compressing the facial (trigeminal) nerve. This condition causes patients to suffer extreme facial pain and unusual sensitivity to pressure and touch.

## What this video covers

- **What trigeminal neuralgia feels like** — sudden, severe, electric-shock-like facial pain, often triggered by touch, chewing, or even a breeze
- **What causes it** — a blood vessel compressing the trigeminal nerve near the brainstem
- **How MVD relieves it** — carefully separating the vessel from the nerve rather than damaging the nerve itself
- **Why early diagnosis matters** — an MRI can confirm vascular compression and rule out other causes

## Key takeaways

> Early diagnosis makes a significant difference to treatment outcomes. If you or a family member are experiencing persistent or worsening facial pain, please seek evaluation promptly.

### When should I be concerned?

The symptoms below warrant prompt neurological assessment:

- Facial pain that is new and progressively worsening
- Sudden facial weakness or drooping
- Hearing loss
- Morning headaches accompanying facial symptoms

## Frequently asked questions

**What is microvascular decompression (MVD) surgery?**
Microvascular decompression is a surgical procedure that repositions a blood vessel that is compressing a cranial nerve, relieving the pressure that causes the nerve to misfire. It is most commonly performed for trigeminal neuralgia, where a blood vessel is found pressing on the trigeminal nerve near the brainstem.

**What causes trigeminal neuralgia?**
Trigeminal neuralgia is most often caused by a blood vessel compressing the trigeminal nerve at the point where it exits the brainstem, wearing away its protective coating over time. This causes sudden, severe, electric-shock-like facial pain, often triggered by light touch, chewing, or even a breeze on the face.

**Is MVD surgery effective for trigeminal neuralgia?**
Yes, for well-selected patients with a confirmed vascular compression, microvascular decompression offers among the highest long-term pain relief rates of any treatment for trigeminal neuralgia, and unlike ablative procedures, it does not intentionally damage the nerve.

**How is trigeminal neuralgia diagnosed?**
Diagnosis is based on the characteristic pattern of sudden, severe, one-sided facial pain, supported by an MRI to look for a blood vessel compressing the trigeminal nerve and to rule out other causes such as a tumour or multiple sclerosis.
